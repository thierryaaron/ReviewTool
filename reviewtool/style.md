# Style guide for the writing reviewers

Edit this file to change what the writing reviewers look for, or pass another file with `--style`.
The manuscript is an academic paper, so judge it as one. Field conventions such as hedged claims,
citations in parentheses and technical vocabulary are expected and are not faults.

## Clarity

- Each sentence should be understood on first reading by a researcher from a neighbouring field.
- Every technical term, acronym and symbol is explained where it first appears, and an idea is introduced before the text relies on it.
- Each term keeps one meaning. Flag a word used for two different things, and two words used for the same thing.
- Prefer describing what happened to naming it with an abstract noun.
- Flag sentences that carry too many clauses, bury the main verb or stack nouns.
- Flag pronouns and phrases such as "this" or "these data" whose referent is unclear.
- Flag claims hedged twice or stated through a double negative ("not insignificant").

## Naturalness

Text reads as natural when each sentence grows out of the one before and the rhythm is that of
someone explaining their work. Flag these patterns, which make prose read as formulaic or
machine-written, when they recur:

- "Not X but Y", "not only X but also Y" and "rather than X, Y" used as a default way to make a point.
- Lists of three where the third item is padding.
- Sentences that announce what is coming instead of saying it ("There are two reasons for this").
- Signposting and self-commentary ("it is worth noting", "importantly", "to be clear", "X matters because").
- Novelty and emphasis words that are not argued for ("unprecedented", "crucial", "remarkable", "landmark").
- Colons and dashes used for dramatic effect, and semicolons joining sentences that could stand alone.
- Strings of short declarative sentences that are logically linked and should be joined with "because", "so" or "which".
- Paragraphs that all follow the same template, for example claim, three examples, summarising line.
- Closing sentences that restate the paragraph or reach for a moral.
- Vocabulary that is ornate where a plain word would do.

One instance of a pattern is rarely worth reporting. Report a pattern when it recurs, with examples.

## What to leave alone

- Prose that is clear and correct, even where you would have phrased it differently.
- The authors' choices between acceptable variants of spelling, punctuation and citation style, provided they are consistent.
- Long sentences that are easy to follow.
